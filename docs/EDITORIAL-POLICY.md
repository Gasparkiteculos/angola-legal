# Editorial policy

## Record what the evidence supports

Prefer primary and official sources for identity and publication details. Secondary sources may help discovery, but mark claims based on them and seek corroboration. Record source URL/name and access date where available.

## Separate metadata dimensions

Never derive legal force from document nature, temporal status from the latest date found, verification from official appearance, or reuse permission from public access. Each field must have its own evidence and may remain unknown.

## Verification states

- `verified`: key identity and cited metadata checked against reliable evidence.
- `partially_verified`: some fields checked; material uncertainty remains.
- `unverified`: not yet checked.
- `disputed`: credible conflict or challenge exists; explain it in notes.
- `withdrawn`: record should no longer be relied on; preserve correction history where appropriate.

A verification label does not certify legal validity or provide legal advice.

## Temporal status

Use `in_force`, `partially_in_force`, `amended`, `repealed`, `expired`, `not_yet_in_force`, `historical`, `not_applicable`, or `unknown` only when supported. Distinguish amendment history from present effect. Include dates and related identifiers where evidence permits.

## Corrections

Explain corrections and cite evidence. Preserve a record of consequential changes through repository history or linked issue. Do not silently convert a contested assertion into certainty.

## Language and style

Keep titles faithful to sources. Project-authored summaries should be neutral, concise, and clearly distinguished from source text. Do not reproduce long excerpts without confirmed rights.
